# AFL rule changes 2015 to 2027, and news sources for team news

Prepared 26 October 2026. Reading only: nothing was signed up for, paid for, sent or scraped.

## How to read this document

**Every source here was read through search-engine results, not by opening the page.** This environment's network proxy refused direct connections to afl.com.au, wikipedia.org and every other site tried, so the pages themselves could not be opened. Each fact below comes from the title, URL and excerpt a web search returned. That is good enough to say what a page claims. It is not as strong as reading the whole page, and a few excerpts disagree with each other (flagged where they do). Before a figure goes into the model as a hard-coded constant, open the source once in a browser.

Source strength grades used throughout:

| Grade | Meaning |
|---|---|
| A | Official: AFL.com.au, an AFL club site, the AFL annual report, Champion Data's own documentation, or the vendor's own pricing or terms page |
| B | Major media or a named analyst: ABC, ESPN, SEN, The Age/Herald Sun syndication, Zero Hanger, Shannon Gill |
| C | Secondary: blogs, aggregators, vendor comparison sites, fan sites, community rule summaries |
| (snippet) | Added when only a search excerpt was seen. It applies to every row in this document unless the row says otherwise |

"Unknown" means the search did not find it. It does not mean the thing does not exist.

### The substitute: in (2011 to 2015), out (2016 to 2020), medical (2021 to 2022), tactical (2023 to 2025), out again (2026)

**Your belief is right: the substitute was removed for 2026.** Clubs name 23: 18 on the field and five on an interchange bench, with the rotation cap unchanged at 75. ([AFL.com.au](https://www.afl.com.au/news/1435389/league-makes-two-major-changes-in-overhaul), A; [The Nightly](https://thenightly.com.au/sport/afl/afl-confirms-centre-bounce-gone-and-substitution-rule-scrapped-in-major-rules-shake-up-c-20199272), B). One excerpt of an Opening Round teams listing described sides as "22 named players plus emergencies" ([Flashscore](https://www.flashscore.com/news/aussie-rules-afl-afl-teams-opening-round/vqDd0q0s), C), which conflicts with 23; the AFL wording is the stronger source.

Correction worth knowing for a model trained from 2012: **there were FOUR sub regimes in your training window, not one.** 2012 to 2015 had a free-use sub with a three-player bench; 2016 to 2020 had no sub and a four-player bench; 2021 and 2022 a medical-only sub; 2023 to 2025 a tactical sub; 2026 none, with a five-player bench. In every sub season some players have a partial game (subbed on or off), which drags their counts down and is not a form signal.

### Kick-in changes (2019, 2021, 2026)

2019 play on without kicking to yourself and the mark at 10 m; 2021 the mark at 15 m; 2026 eight seconds to restart after a behind (was 12). The aim in 2019 was more scoring; the aim in 2026 was cutting dead time. No measured effect on kick-ins, rebound 50s or marks found (unknown). Sources as in the timeline.

### The 2026 package as a whole

**Your belief is right: the centre bounce was replaced by a ball-up (the umpire throws the ball up), at centre restarts and everywhere else.** The AFL gave umpire health and safety and alignment with AFLW (thrown up since 2017) as reasons ([ESPN](https://www.espn.com/afl/story/_/id/46442874/afl-centre-bounce-scrapped-substitute-rule-removed-changes), B; [AFL.com.au](https://www.afl.com.au/news/1435389/league-makes-two-major-changes-in-overhaul), A). The two structural changes (bounce, sub) were announced first; the seven-change package followed on about 28 to 29 October 2025, aimed at cutting about three minutes of match length ([ABC](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2025-10-29/afl-confirms-last-disposal-new-rule-changes-2026-season/105947430), B). A Carlton release lists only four of the seven as interpretations, which is a labelling difference, not a contradiction ([Carlton FC](https://www.carltonfc.com.au/news/1923133/afl-announces-seven-rule-interpretation-changes-for-2026), A).

## 2.2 Recommendation

**I would use two things, and keep AFL.com.au out of the automated pipeline.**

1. **Google News RSS, Australian edition, as the alert layer** (free). One search query per club and one per player on a watchlist (for example `"Bontempelli" AFL`, or `"Carlton" (managed OR omitted OR "will play" OR tag)`), polled every 15 to 30 minutes on team-news days (Tuesday injury lists, Thursday selections, game-day late changes). It covers more Australian outlets than any API here, including the ones that break selection news. The cost is the terms: it is built for personal reading, and whether a polling script is allowed depends on Google's robots.txt and terms, which I could not open. Check robots.txt for `/rss` before running it, poll gently, store only headline, outlet, time and link.
   - If you would rather have a contract than a judgement call, swap this for **GNews Essential** (about EUR 49.99 a month, real time, full content claimed). Read its terms page first; the commercial-use wording was reported inconsistently.
2. **The Guardian Open Platform for full text** (free key). It is the only source here whose free tier gives the whole article body under clear developer terms, so it is the one place your pipeline can read "X will play forward" in context. Its AFL volume is small, so it is a supplement, not the backbone.

**Not recommended:** NewsAPI.org (free plan is development only and 24 hours late, so it cannot catch Thursday's teams before Friday's game, and the production plan is USD 449 a month); NewsData.io and GNews free tiers (12-hour delay); Reddit (approval now required, and fan threads mostly restate AFL.com.au, which brings its terms question with it); Squiggle (no news at all, though it is still the right free source for fixtures and tips).

## 2.3 What the pipeline would realistically get

| Layer | Realistic content | Good enough for |
|---|---|---|
| Google News RSS / ABC RSS / free API tiers | Headline, outlet, time, one or two sentence summary at most | Flags: "X out (hamstring)", "Y managed", "Z recalled". Headlines carry most outs and returns |
| Guardian body text, paid GNews/NewsData | Whole article | Role detail: "will play forward", "tagging role", "spent time in the ruck", which is usually in paragraph four, not the headline |
| Official team sheets (AFL.com.au, Thursday evening, and final changes before the game) | Structured ins, outs, bench | The single most valuable input, and the one whose terms (per your note) forbid storing. Options: read it by hand and key the few changes in, or license it |

Expect the automated layer to catch most injuries, omissions and recalls from headlines within an hour of publication, and to catch role changes only where a full-text source happens to cover that club that week. Role news ("tag", "forward", "burst midfielder off the bench") is the hardest to automate and, under the 2026 five-man bench, matters more than before: a midfielder used as a bench burst plays fewer minutes even when named.
